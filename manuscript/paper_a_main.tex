\begin{abstract}
Ecological management often reuses coarse state classifications after species turnover, interaction rewiring, habitat change, or changes in the available interventions. A classification that was adequate when it was defined can then become insufficient for a new management decision even if all model parameters are known perfectly. We formalize this problem as an audit of the decision sufficiency of an inherited ecological state variable. A source classification is carried to a target system through a declared structural relation and tested against target outputs, legal actions, and action-conditioned successors. Failure returns a local obstruction: states that share an inherited label but can no longer be treated interchangeably. Established coarsest-refinement machinery then supplies the least exact state distinction required by the target interface, and the resulting increase in state count defines a structural repair burden. We show that this structural burden is a distinct quantity from conditional label information and from decision regret, and that no universal ordering relates them. A route-coherence result identifies when several declared replacement routes admit one route-independent inherited interface and when historical context must instead remain explicit. An illustrative plant--pollinator decision example shows how the missing distinction can reverse restoration priority. Because the audit returns the obstruction pairs that a monitoring system would have to separate, we also record, as an application rather than a theorem of the present framework, how those pairs translate into a measurement-library feasibility question; deciding that question under detection error, cost, and risk belongs to a separate evidence-contract layer.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} ecological state classification; adaptive management; structural change; targeted monitoring; model transfer; decision sufficiency

\section{Introduction}
Ecological management depends on compressed descriptions of complex systems. Vegetation states, habitat classes, functional guilds, occupancy states, resilience categories, and other macrostates allow monitoring and intervention to proceed without representing every species, interaction, demographic coordinate, and historical detail. State-and-transition models make this compression explicit by linking ecological states to expected pathways of change and management actions, and empirical work has shown how such models can support targeted monitoring by identifying a reduced set of variables that distinguish predefined states and transitions \citep{JonesEtAl2023}. Adaptive-management frameworks likewise require a state representation on which action consequences can be evaluated \citep{Holling1978,Walters1986}.

The difficulty is that the adequacy of a state classification need not survive ecological change. Species can disappear or colonize, interaction networks can rewire, habitats can be replaced, and management can introduce interventions that were unavailable when the classification was designed. Two configurations that were safely merged under the source regime can then differ in an output that matters, in which actions are feasible, or in where the same action leads. In that case the problem is not merely that a transition probability or regression coefficient needs to be re-estimated. The inherited state variable itself suppresses a distinction required by the target decision.

Ecologists have identified transferability as a persistent challenge for prediction across places and conditions \citep{YatesEtAl2018}. Most familiar transfer problems are statistical: whether fitted relationships remain accurate, how extrapolation should be diagnosed, or how uncertainty should be propagated. The failure considered here is deliberately prior to those questions. It can occur with perfect knowledge of both source and target systems and with no sampling or parameter error. The question is structural:
\begin{quote}
When does an ecological state classification that was accepted for a source management problem remain sufficient after structural change, and, when it fails, what must a monitoring system be able to distinguish before that state representation can again support the target decision?
\end{quote}

Formal work already supplies much of the mathematical machinery needed to answer parts of this question. Lumpability and bisimulation characterize exact aggregation within a specified process \citep{KemenySnell1960,FeretEtAl2012,LarsenSkou1991}. Coarsest stable refinement from an arbitrary initial partition is established in partition-refinement theory and MDP model minimization \citep{PaigeTarjan1987,GivanDeanGreig2003}. State abstraction has been systematized by what it preserves \citep{LiWalshLittman2006}, maps between distinct decision processes are treated by homomorphism theory \citep{RavindranBarto2003}, and state abstractions have been explicitly transferred from source MDPs to target MDPs \citep{WalshLiLittman2006Transfer}. We therefore claim none of these constructions as new.

The ecological gap is narrower but operationally important. Managers often inherit a classification that already has an accepted interpretation and a monitoring infrastructure. After a declared structural change, the relevant question is not simply how to compute another efficient abstraction of the target. It is whether that inherited interface still supports the target actions, where it fails if it does not, and whether the measurements available in practice can recover the missing distinction. This differs from targeted monitoring that begins with predefined states and asks which measured variables best discriminate them \citep{JonesEtAl2023}: here the first question is whether the predefined states themselves remain decision-sufficient. It also differs from value-of-information analysis, which asks whether reducing uncertainty is worth the cost given objectives, actions, and consequences \citep{CanessaEtAl2015}; our audit can identify a structurally missing distinction and test whether a candidate measurement library can express it before assigning uncertainty or sampling value.

We organize these ingredients into an ecological audit of inherited semantics. A source macro-law is carried to a target system through a declared relation between state spaces that need not coincide. The inherited target fibers are tested for equality of current output, legal-action rows, and successor labels. If they pass, the old state variable remains exact for the declared target interface. If they fail, a pair of states provides a local obstruction, and established coarsest-refinement machinery identifies the least exact split of the inherited classes. We then separate three quantities that a single ``repair cost'' would conflate: the structural increase in state count, the conditional information the repair adds under a target-state distribution, and the decision regret of continuing to use the inherited classification. Because the audit returns a finite set of state pairs that have ceased to be interchangeable, it also states what a monitoring system would have to distinguish; we develop that translation as an application, and leave its resolution under detection error, cost, and risk to a separate evidence-contract layer.

Three secondary results delimit what this repair does and does not mean. First, structural state-count defect is separated from distribution-sensitive label information and management regret. Second, a finite decision example shows that even one hidden distinction can reverse restoration priority. Third, when several declared replacement routes reach the same target system, historical context is retained only when the routes carry incompatible terminal meanings. The result is best viewed as a theory of ecological state representation and monitoring repair under structural change: before optimizing a policy or collecting more data, test whether the state variable supplied to that decision still contains, and can practically recover, the distinctions that the target intervention exposes.

\subsection{Exact aggregation and refinement}
Lumpability and behavioral equivalence provide exact standards for when a coarse variable factors the behavior of a fixed process \citep{KemenySnell1960,FeretEtAl2012,LarsenSkou1991}. The construction used below for repair is likewise established. The relational coarsest-partition problem accepts an arbitrary starting partition \citep{PaigeTarjan1987}, and MDP model minimization computes the coarsest homogeneous refinement of any partition in a stochastic setting that contains our deterministic case \citep{GivanDeanGreig2003}. Consequently, finite termination, coarseness, and uniqueness of that refinement are not Paper A discoveries. We nevertheless give a self-contained finite specialization in the supplement so the manuscript has no hidden proof dependency.

\subsection{Abstraction transfer between systems}
Source-to-target reuse is also not new in itself. Homomorphism theory relates distinct decision processes under dynamics-preserving maps \citep{RavindranBarto2003}, and \citet{WalshLiLittman2006Transfer} explicitly develop an algorithm for transferring state abstractions learned from source MDPs to target MDPs. Their objective is efficient planning and learning in target environments under transferable abstraction structure. Our ecological question is different but narrower: a classification with an accepted source meaning is carried through a declared ecological correspondence, and we ask whether the target management repertoire falsifies that inherited merge. The correspondence need not be a valid homomorphism; detecting its failure with respect to the inherited interface is precisely the audit.

\subsection{Ecological state models, monitoring, and decision analysis}
State-and-transition models provide a natural applied setting because they connect ecological state labels to transitions, drivers, and management expectations. \citet{JonesEtAl2023} show how field data can validate expert-defined vegetation states and identify a small number of variables and thresholds for targeted monitoring. We address the complementary upstream problem: structural change may make two configurations within one inherited state respond differently to a target intervention, so the state definition itself must first be audited. Once the audit returns obstruction pairs, empirical indicator-selection methods can search for measurements and thresholds that separate precisely those required distinctions.

Decision analysis addresses a downstream question. Value-of-information methods quantify whether collecting additional data is worth its cost given uncertainty, objectives, actions, and consequences \citep{CanessaEtAl2015}. The present framework does not calculate VoI. It first asks whether the current state representation can express the action consequence at all and whether the supplied candidate measurements can reconstruct the repaired state. If they can, their acquisition costs and uncertainties can then enter a VoI analysis.

\subsection{Historical context}
Ecological legacy effects motivate retaining disturbance, colonization, or management history, but retaining all history can make a state description unnecessarily large. Formal computer-science literatures likewise contain history-preserving equivalences that retain causal information about executions \citep{MontanariPistore1997}. We do not claim invention of history-sensitive state descriptions. Our scoped question is whether several declared structural-replacement routes carry the same source label assignment to a shared terminal system. If they do, route identity can be discarded for this inherited interface; if they do not, a route-free carried label does not exist.

\subsection{Open-future interface lower bounds}
One further boundary needs stating explicitly, because the two questions are easy to conflate. A neighboring problem asks whether each fixed closed future grammar may admit its own small optimal exact interface while the minimum exact interface for a jointly open future grammar is necessarily much larger. That question optimizes the closed interfaces independently, so no inherited partition is fixed anywhere in it. The audit developed here has the opposite constraint: one accepted source classification is fixed first, carried to the target, and every admissible repair must refine its inherited labels. A target grammar may enlarge and thereby expose an obstruction pair, but that is an instance of source-relative transport, not the independently optimized closed-versus-open separation. We do not claim that separation here.

\section{Operational framework}
A finite controlled ecological system is a tuple
\[
\mathcal{S}=(X,A,G,\delta,y),
\]
where $X$ is the state set, $A$ is the declared action set, $G$ is a finite legal-action grammar, $\delta$ is the controlled transition rule, and $y$ is the observable output. A macro-law is a surjection $\pi:X\times G\to Q$.

\begin{definition}[Exact operational macro-law]
A macro-law is exact when states in the same macro-fiber have identical current outputs, identical legal-action rows, and successors carrying the same macro-label under every declared legal action.
\end{definition}

Structural change from a source system $\mathcal{S}_0$ to a target system $\mathcal{S}_1$ is represented by a declared total relation
\[
R\subseteq (X_0\times G_0)\times (X_1\times G_1).
\]
When the relation is source-label consistent and covers the target, the source law induces a carried target classification $C=\pi_R$. The relation is treated as declared scientific input; this paper does not infer it from data. The target may also enlarge the legal action grammar, so newly legal actions can expose distinctions inside an inherited fiber. Such enlargement enters here only as an instance of source-relative transport, with the inherited law fixed and constraining admissible repair; it is not the cross-grammar problem in which closed interfaces are optimized independently before comparison with a jointly open minimum.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Audit the inherited state before reusing it}
\begin{theorem}[Operational portability]
For a finite exact source macro-law and a declared label-consistent structural-change relation, the carried target classification is exact if and only if current outputs, legal-action rows, and successor carried labels are constant inside every carried target fiber.
\end{theorem}

This criterion is the audit. It asks whether the old state variable still contains every distinction required by the declared target outputs and interventions. Failure is structural and therefore cannot be repaired merely by re-estimating parameters while keeping the same state partition.

\begin{proposition}[Local obstruction]
Failure of portability is witnessed by two target states in one inherited fiber that differ in current output, legal actions, or successor inherited labels under a declared action or finite legal future.
\end{proposition}

A witness identifies a concrete contrast that the inherited state variable suppresses. If the difference is caused, for example, by substitute-pollinator access, soil condition, or disturbance legacy, the monitoring question is no longer ``which variables best describe the system?'' but ``which measurements can distinguish every contrast that the target decision requires?''

Let $Q^\ast$ denote the coarsest exact target partition refining the carried classification $C$.

\begin{proposition}[Source-relative exact repair]
Applying the standard coarsest stable-refinement construction to $C$ yields $Q^\ast$, the unique coarsest exact target partition that never merges states separated by the inherited classification.
\end{proposition}

This is a finite specialization of established partition-refinement results \citep{PaigeTarjan1987,GivanDeanGreig2003}, not a new generic refinement theorem. A complete specialization proof is given in the supplement; the citations establish prior-art status and algorithmic context.

\subsection{Application: obstruction pairs as a measurement requirement}
The audit above is self-contained: it returns whether the inherited classification is still decision-sufficient and, if not, the least exact repair. This subsection is an application of that output rather than a further result about inherited semantics, and we mark the boundary explicitly. The audit determines \emph{which distinctions} a monitoring system would have to resolve. Whether a particular measurement library can resolve them, at what cost, and under what detection error and false-report risk, is a question about an evidence contract rather than about inherited semantics, and it is not answered here. We record only the exact deterministic translation from audit output to measurement requirement, because that translation is what makes the audit actionable; the generic separation and covering formulations it reduces to are long established, and we claim neither them nor a coupling theorem between the two layers.

Define the obstruction-pair set
\[
E=\{(x,x'):C(x)=C(x'),\;Q^\ast(x)\ne Q^\ast(x')\}.
\]
Suppose a candidate monitoring library contains measurements $z_j:X\to Z_j$, $j\in J$. For a selected set $S\subseteq J$, write $z_S(x)=(z_j(x))_{j\in S}$.

\begin{definition}[Exact monitoring realization]
A selected measurement set $S$ realizes the repaired classification relative to $C$ when there exists a decoder $d_S$ such that
\[
Q^\ast(x)=d_S(C(x),z_S(x))
\]
for every target state $x$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Monitoring realization criterion]
A selected measurement set $S$ realizes $Q^\ast$ relative to $C$ if and only if every obstruction pair is separated by at least one selected measurement:
\[
\forall (x,x')\in E\;\exists j\in S:
\quad z_j(x)\ne z_j(x').
\]
\end{proposition}

The condition is both necessary and sufficient, not merely heuristic. If a pair in $E$ survives all selected measurements, the same inherited label and measurement vector would have to decode to two different repaired states. Conversely, if every obstruction pair is separated, equality of $(C,z_S)$ can never join two different $Q^\ast$ classes, so the decoder is well defined. The full proof is given in the supplement.

For each candidate measurement let
\[
A_j=\{(x,x')\in E:z_j(x)\ne z_j(x')\}
\]
be the obstruction pairs that it separates.

\begin{corollary}[Monitoring feasibility and minimum cost]
An exact realization exists within the supplied measurement library if and only if
\[
\bigcup_{j\in J}A_j=E.
\]
When it exists and measurement $j$ has acquisition cost $c_j$, the least-cost exact realization is
\[
\min_{S\subseteq J}\sum_{j\in S}c_j
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\bigcup_{j\in S}A_j=E.
\]
\end{corollary}

Thus the audit can return three practically distinct outputs. If the inherited classification passes, monitoring need not be redesigned for the declared interface. If it fails but the candidate library covers $E$, the framework identifies a least-cost exact measurement subset. If some obstruction pair is outside $\cup_j A_j$, the library itself is structurally inadequate: no combination of the supplied measurements can reconstruct the repaired state exactly.

\subsection{A missing state distinction can reverse restoration priority}
Consider two degraded sites, A and B, containing the same focal plant and showing the same coarse pollination output. Under the source monitoring and action repertoire, both are classified as ``pollination maintained,'' while a third state represents failure of pollination service. In the verified finite witness, the inherited target labels are $(0,0,1)$.

Structural change alters the pollinator community. The original dominant pollinator is lost, but one target configuration retains access to a substitute guild whereas the other does not. Competitor removal is introduced as a target intervention that can open floral access around the focal plant. Under this new action, the two states carrying the same inherited label have different successors: Site A lacks the substitute response channel, whereas Site B retains it. The inherited classification is therefore not decision-sufficient for the new intervention. Standard refinement splits only that exposed fiber, changing $(0,0,1)$ to $(0,1,2)$. The ecological interpretation of the split is ``record substitute-pollinator response capacity,'' not ``add one arbitrary state.''

To make the management consequence explicit without presenting invented values as data, let intervention success at Site $i$ have probability $p_i$, success have benefit $B$, and intervention cost be $c_i$. The repaired model prefers Site B exactly when
\[
Bp_B-c_B>Bp_A-c_A,
\qquad\text{or equivalently}\qquad
p_B-p_A>\frac{c_B-c_A}{B}.
\]
An inherited model that pools A and B assigns the same success probability to both and therefore chooses the cheaper site whenever $c_A<c_B$.

For illustration, set $B=1$, $c_A=0.20$, $c_B=0.35$, $p_A=0.25$, and $p_B=0.80$. The inherited pooled probability is $\bar p=0.525$, giving expected net benefits $0.325$ for A and $0.175$ for B, so the inherited rule chooses A. The repaired model gives $0.05$ for A and $0.45$ for B, so it chooses B. Evaluated under the repaired consequences, using the inherited choice incurs one-step regret $0.40$. These numbers are illustrative, not empirical estimates; their purpose is to identify the quantities a field application would need to estimate and the parameter region in which a structural split changes management.

\subsection{Repair complexity, information, and decision loss are different quantities}
The structural repair itself can be summarized by
\[
\Delta_{\#}=|Q^\ast|-|C|,
\qquad
\Delta_K=\log_2|Q^\ast|-\log_2|C|.
\]
These quantities describe loss of compression only. In the verified accumulating family with $m$ independently exposed binary distinctions,
\[
|C|=2,\qquad |Q^\ast|=2^m+1,
\]
so $\Delta_{\#}=2^m-1$. This is a sharp finite witness about structural representation complexity, not a universal ecological scaling law.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{../artifacts/figure2_transport_defect.svg}
\caption{Structural repair complexity in the verified sharp finite family with independently exposed target distinctions. The curve concerns state-space compression only and is not a claim about monitoring cost or decision regret}
\label{fig:defect}
\end{figure}

If a target-state distribution $\mu$ is supplied, let $C$ and $Q^\ast$ also denote the inherited and repaired labels of a target state drawn from $\mu$.

\begin{definition}[Conditional repair information]
Define
\[
D_H(\mu)=H_\mu(Q^\ast\mid C).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Minimum conditional label information]
For any exact target refinement $P$ of $C$,
\[
H_\mu(Q^\ast\mid C)\le H_\mu(P\mid C).
\]
If $k_c$ repaired blocks occur inside carried class $c$, then
\[
0\le D_H(\mu)
\le \sum_c \Pr_\mu(C=c)\log_2 k_c
\le \log_2\max_c k_c.
\]
\end{proposition}

This is an information-theoretic corollary of coarsest exact repair: unlike $\Delta_K$, it weights the extra state information by the supplied target-state distribution.

To separate representation complexity from management consequence, let $U$ be a finite management-action set and $u(x,a)$ a payoff. For a state representation $P$, define the best value achievable by a decision rule that can use only $P$ as
\[
V_\mu(P;u)=
\max_{\alpha:\operatorname{im}(P)\to U}
\sum_x \mu(x)\,u(x,\alpha(P(x))).
\]
The regret of retaining the inherited state instead of the repaired state is
\[
\mathcal{R}_\mu(C,Q^\ast;u)
=
V_\mu(Q^\ast;u)-V_\mu(C;u).
\]

\begin{proposition}[No universal defect--regret ordering]
\label{prop:defect-regret}
For every integer $d\ge1$ and every real $r\ge0$, there exists a finite target operational system for which $Q^\ast$ is the coarsest exact repair of $C$ and
\[
|Q^\ast|-|C|=d,
\qquad
\mathcal{R}_\mu(C,Q^\ast;u)=r.
\]
\end{proposition}

Hence state-count defect alone gives neither a nontrivial universal upper bound nor a positive universal lower bound on management regret and cannot universally rank ecological consequence. The construction is given in the supplement. This is why monitoring cost, conditional information, and decision loss are reported separately rather than treated as interpretations of $\Delta_{\#}$.

\subsection{Historical context is retained only when routes change inherited meaning}
Consider a rooted directed acyclic graph of declared replacement relations. Every root-to-terminal route carries the source label map to the terminal system.

\begin{theorem}[Route coherence]
A single route-independent carried classification, repaired classification, and structural defect exist if and only if every root-to-terminal route induces the same terminal carried label map.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Minimum carried-map context]
When terminal carried maps disagree, one immutable context per distinct carried terminal map is necessary and sufficient to preserve all declared routes before exact refinement.
\end{theorem}

These results concern the semantics carried by externally declared replacement routes. They are not claims that history-sensitive equivalence is new in general \citep{MontanariPistore1997}. Their ecological interpretation is narrower: disturbance or management history should be retained only when alternative declared routes assign incompatible operational meanings to the same terminal configurations.

\section{Discussion}
The central result is not a new generic state-reduction algorithm. It is a change in where ecological model transfer is audited and what the audit returns. Ecological workflows commonly ask whether a model can be refitted, extrapolated, or updated after environmental change. Here the prior question is whether the state variable supplied to that workflow remains sufficient for the target intervention. Structural change can expose a distinction that an inherited class was designed to ignore. When that happens, better parameter estimates within the old state representation do not solve the problem.

The audit is also what makes the failure actionable. A failed state audit does not merely say that ``more monitoring'' is needed: it returns a finite set of pairs that must become distinguishable, which is a concrete design requirement rather than a request for effort. Turning that requirement into a monitoring decision is a further step and a different problem. Once a candidate measurement library is supplied, asking whether it separates the required pairs, and at what cost, is a question about an evidence contract; answering it responsibly also requires detection error, thresholds, sampling effort, and false-report risk, none of which the present exact framework supplies. We therefore state the translation and stop there, rather than presenting evidence-layer design as a result of the semantic audit.

This relationship makes the boundary with existing targeted monitoring precise. \citet{JonesEtAl2023} begin with ecological states that are already specified and identify field variables and thresholds that discriminate them. Paper A asks an upstream question: after structural or intervention change, are those inherited states still valid for the management problem? Only if the answer is no does the framework generate a new set of distinctions that the subsequent monitoring design must resolve. The two approaches therefore compose naturally rather than compete.

The distinction from value of information is similarly sequential. VoI asks whether learning about an uncertain state, parameter, or model will improve expected management outcomes enough to justify sampling \citep{CanessaEtAl2015}. Our audit asks whether the current state representation is structurally capable of expressing the relevant action consequence and whether a proposed measurement library can reconstruct the necessary state. Once feasible measurements, their costs, uncertainty, and decision consequences are specified, VoI becomes the appropriate downstream tool.

Separating structural defect from decision regret prevents an attractive but unsupported interpretation. A one-state split can be management-critical if it reverses a high-stakes intervention, while a large state-count expansion can be nearly irrelevant if the exposed distinctions are rare or have negligible consequences. The stronger construction in Proposition~\ref{prop:defect-regret} shows that any positive defect can coexist with any nonnegative regret after suitable finite decision consequences are specified. $\Delta_{\#}$ and $\Delta_K$ therefore describe loss of compression, not ecological importance.

Historical context should be treated with the same economy. Ecology contains many legacy effects, but a model need not record a complete disturbance history simply because history can matter. The route-coherence criterion asks a more specific question: do alternative declared histories change the inherited operational meaning assigned to the present configuration? If not, history can be discarded for this decision interface. If so, the completion retains only the distinct carried-map contexts required to make the present state unambiguous.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}
The framework is appropriate when investigators can declare a finite or finitely represented source system, a target system, the outputs and interventions that matter, and a defensible relation carrying source configurations to target configurations. Concrete uses could include auditing restoration-priority classes after interaction turnover, testing whether habitat or resilience categories remain decision-sufficient after disturbance, or checking whether an inherited community-state interface can support a newly available intervention.

It should not be used as a substitute for estimating transition probabilities, detecting species, inferring causal effects, learning the source--target relation, optimizing a policy, or quantifying observation and model uncertainty. Exact monitoring realization assumes the candidate measurements are deterministic functions of the target state. Imperfect detection, measurement error, stochastic transition dynamics, and uncertainty in the structural relation are extensions rather than results established here.

The plant--pollinator case is a finite theoretical example. Its deterministic split is verified by the repository witnesses, and its probability/cost layer is an illustrative sensitivity calculation. It is not evidence that a particular plant, pollinator guild, or restoration program exhibits the stated probabilities or priority reversal.

The central ecological implication is therefore specific: a management state is not permanently valid merely because its label remains interpretable. Its adequacy depends on the distinctions exposed by the current system and intervention repertoire. When structural change makes an inherited state insufficient, the next scientific question is whether available measurements can reconstruct the missing distinction. That converts state-classification failure into a falsifiable monitoring-design requirement rather than an invitation to collect more variables indiscriminately.

\section{Methods and proof organization}
The portability criterion follows by necessity and sufficiency of output, legal-row, and successor uniformity within carried fibers. Local obstruction is the corresponding finite counter-witness. The source-relative repair uses the standard monotone partition-refinement construction \citep{PaigeTarjan1987,GivanDeanGreig2003}; the supplement defines its exact signature and gives the finite specialization proof. The monitoring-realization theorem is proved by equivalence between existence of a decoder from $(C,z_S)$ and pairwise separation of all obstruction pairs; the minimum-cost corollary is then weighted set cover. The conditional-information inequality follows from the fact that every exact refinement of $C$ refines $Q^\ast$. The defect--regret result is proved constructively for arbitrary defect $d\ge1$ and regret $r\ge0$. Route coherence is equality of complete terminal carried maps across declared routes; minimum context follows because distinct carried maps cannot share one immutable route-free label assignment. Detailed proofs, finite witnesses, and reproducibility commands are provided in the supplementary manuscript and repository workflows.
