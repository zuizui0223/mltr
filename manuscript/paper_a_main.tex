\begin{abstract}
Ecological management relies on compressed state descriptions whose usefulness depends on the decisions they support. A classification that was sufficient before species turnover, interaction rewiring, or a change in the intervention repertoire can therefore remain descriptively familiar while becoming decision-insufficient. We formulate a finite operational audit for reusing an accepted source macro-law after declared structural replacement. A many-to-many replacement relation carries one inherited label map exactly when no target state receives incompatible source labels. Along a fixed replacement chain, direct and stepwise carriage are simultaneously well defined and, when defined, yield the same terminal inherited map; decomposing one route therefore does not itself create historical dependence. Genuine route dependence begins when distinct declared replacement histories induce different complete carried terminal maps. A single route-independent inherited law exists exactly when those maps agree. When they disagree, histories can share an immutable mode exactly when their complete maps are equal, so one mode per distinct map is necessary and sufficient to preserve all declared inherited meanings. Standard exactness and partition refinement are used only after inherited semantics have been fixed, to diagnose whether the carried state remains sufficient for target outputs and interventions and to identify the additional distinctions required when it does not. A finite plant--pollinator example shows how a target intervention can reverse restoration priority and how the resulting split points directly to a missing monitoring variable. The contribution is therefore an ecological model-reuse audit built around carried semantics, route coherence, and minimum historical context, not a new generic partition-refinement algorithm.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} ecological model transfer; structural replacement; decision sufficiency; route coherence; historical context; adaptive management

\section{Introduction}
Ecological management rarely retains every species identity, interaction, demographic state, and management history. Instead, it works through compressed state variables: occupancy classes, functional guilds, successional stages, resilience categories, habitat states, or ecosystem regimes. State-and-transition approaches make this role explicit by linking ecological states and transitions to management actions and monitoring information \citep{BestelmeyerEtAl2017}. Adaptive-management frameworks likewise require a state representation on which intervention consequences can be evaluated \citep{Holling1978,Walters1986}. A useful ecological state is therefore not merely a descriptive grouping. It is an information interface between monitoring, prediction, and action.

That interface can become obsolete when ecological structure changes. Species turn over, interaction networks rewire, habitats are modified, climates shift, and management itself changes the set of actions that can be applied. Under such change, two configurations that were previously safe to merge may respond differently to a newly feasible intervention, may admit different actions, or may lead to different successor states. Re-estimating transition parameters is then insufficient: the inherited state variable itself no longer contains the information needed for the target decision.

This creates a practical model-reuse question distinct from ordinary fitting:
\begin{quote}
Given an ecological state classification already accepted for a source system, when can that inherited meaning be carried through structural replacement and remain sufficient for target management, and what additional ecological or historical information is required when it cannot?
\end{quote}
We use \emph{decision sufficiency} in an operational sense: configurations assigned one inherited state must agree on the declared target outputs, feasible management actions, and action-conditioned successor meanings relevant to the management problem. A classification may therefore be adequate for observation alone yet fail as soon as the intervention repertoire changes.

The mathematical machinery required after a target classification has been specified is mature. Lumpability asks when an aggregation preserves a process within a specified stochastic or dynamical system \citep{KemenySnell1960,FeretEtAl2012}. Bisimulation and partition-refinement methods construct behaviorally stable quotients \citep{Milner1989,LarsenSkou1991,PaigeTarjan1987}. The relational coarsest-partition problem explicitly starts from an initial partition and asks for its coarsest compatible refinement \citep{Fernandez1990}. Abstract-interpretation approaches similarly characterize minimal refinements required for strong preservation and their fixed-point computation \citep{RanzatoTapparo2007}. Even in biological-network reduction, an arbitrary user-specified initial partition can be supplied so that designated distinctions are preserved while the unique coarsest admissible refinement is computed \citep{ArgyrisEtAl2023}. We therefore do not claim that starting from an inherited partition, iteratively splitting it, or obtaining a unique coarsest exact refinement is new.

Our problem begins one layer earlier. The target initial partition is not simply chosen by the analyst: it is supposed to carry the meaning of an accepted source classification through a declared relation between potentially non-nested source and target state spaces. A many-to-many relation can itself make that inheritance ambiguous if one target configuration is connected to source states with different accepted labels. When replacement occurs in stages, the inherited meaning should also be independent of whether a fixed route is represented directly or stepwise. Finally, genuinely different replacement histories may connect the same source and terminal systems while inducing different terminal meanings. These are source-semantic questions that must be settled before standard target refinement can be interpreted as a repair of the inherited ecological state variable.

We formalize this sequence in finite operational models. First, a source label map can be carried through a declared replacement relation exactly when every target configuration receives one inherited source-label value. Second, this carriage is compositional: for consecutive total relations $R$ and $S$, direct carriage through $R;S$ exists exactly when the two sequential carries exist, and the terminal maps are then identical. Thus subdivision of one fixed route cannot by itself create historical dependence. Third, all declared replacement histories support one route-independent inherited terminal law exactly when their complete carried terminal maps agree. If they disagree, the equality classes of those maps are the exact units of historical context: one immutable mode per distinct complete map is necessary and sufficient.

Only after this inherited meaning has been fixed do we invoke standard exactness and refinement. The carried target state is audited against outputs, legal management actions, and action-conditioned successors. A local obstruction identifies where the old state variable merges configurations that differ for the target decision. Established partition-refinement machinery then gives the coarsest exact refinement of the inherited labels. Ecologically, the split is interpreted as a candidate additional monitoring distinction, while the increase in state count or description length is reported as a transport defect. These are diagnostic uses of existing refinement theory, not claims of a new generic refinement theorem.

The ecological value is therefore a disciplined reuse decision. The framework separates four cases that are often conflated: the inherited state meaning cannot even be carried unambiguously through the declared replacement relation; it carries and remains decision-sufficient; it carries but requires an additional target distinction; or genuinely different routes assign incompatible terminal meanings and historical context must be retained. The plant--pollinator example below shows the management consequence: a functional classification adequate under the source intervention set recommends one restoration priority, whereas a target-only intervention exposes substitute-pollinator response capacity and reverses which site should be treated first.

Our claims are deliberately limited. We do not infer source--target relations or histories from data, propose a new generic partition-refinement algorithm, claim generic path dependence as new, or treat transport defect as a new fundamental information measure. Exactness is used as a structural benchmark before sampling error, parameter uncertainty, and approximate prediction are introduced. The contribution is a source-relative ecological model-reuse audit whose formal spine is carriage consistency, compositional transport, route coherence, and the minimum historical context forced when inherited meanings diverge.

\subsection{Coarse graining and lumpability}
Coarse graining is indispensable whenever ecological detail must be reduced to functional groups, occupancy states, trophic compartments, or other aggregate variables. Lumpability sharpens one part of this problem by asking when a proposed partition preserves a process exactly within a fixed system \citep{KemenySnell1960,FeretEtAl2012}. Our decision-sufficiency criterion uses a related exactness standard. The difference is not that MLTR has a special kind of coarsest target partition; it is that one previously accepted source classification is first carried to a structurally changed target and then audited for the target intervention problem.

\subsection{Bisimulation, initial partitions, and abstract refinement}
Bisimulation and related abstraction theories characterize states that agree in observations and future behavior \citep{Milner1989,LarsenSkou1991}. Coarsest-quotient and partition-refinement algorithms are established tools \citep{PaigeTarjan1987}; the relational coarsest-partition formulation already asks for the coarsest compatible refinement of a supplied initial partition \citep{Fernandez1990}. Abstract interpretation similarly provides minimal strongly preserving refinements and fixed-point characterizations \citep{RanzatoTapparo2007}. Consequently, the existence, termination, and uniqueness of the coarsest exact refinement used below are not presented as new results.

The same caution applies to preserving user-selected distinctions. Biological-network reduction can start from an arbitrary initial partition so that specified variables remain distinguishable while a coarsest valid refinement is computed \citep{ArgyrisEtAl2023}. Thus MLTR's source-relative contribution cannot rest merely on the instruction ``do not merge across these initial labels.'' The added object is the source--target relation that creates those inherited target labels. That relation may be non-nested and many-to-many; it can create an inherited-label conflict before refinement begins; its induced label map must compose consistently across stages; and alternative routes can induce incompatible terminal maps.

\subsection{Transportability and model transfer}
Transportability research asks whether conclusions learned in one environment remain valid in another, with causal identifiability and external validity as central concerns \citep{PearlBareinboim2014}. Our framework addresses a prior structural question. It assumes that the source and target systems and their relation have been declared and asks whether the inherited macrostate interface can, even in principle, support target outputs and interventions. It neither estimates the relation nor guarantees empirical transfer. A model may pass the structural audit and still transfer poorly because parameters or observations are uncertain; conversely, parameter refitting cannot make an inherited aggregation exact when a target action sends two states in one inherited class to different successor meanings.

\subsection{Ecological state classifications, state-and-transition models, and adaptive management}
State-and-transition models organize ecological states, transitions, management actions, and contextual information precisely because ecological classifications are used to support decisions \citep{BestelmeyerEtAl2017}. Resilience and regime-shift theory likewise motivates categories of persistence, degradation, and recovery potential \citep{Holling1973,SchefferEtAl2001}. Adaptive management treats interventions as iterative decisions that require a usable state representation \citep{Holling1978,Walters1986}. MLTR operates at the interface among these ideas: it asks whether a state label inherited from an earlier ecological structure still contains enough information for the actions now under consideration. A local obstruction identifies which apparently equivalent configurations respond differently, and the subsequent standard refinement converts that failure into the smallest additional exact distinction relative to the inherited labels.

\subsection{Relation to open-future compression bounds}
A neighboring but distinct problem asks whether each fixed closed future grammar may admit its own small optimal exact interface while the minimum exact interface for a jointly open future grammar is necessarily much larger. That problem optimizes closed interfaces independently and therefore does not fix one inherited source partition. The present paper instead fixes one accepted source meaning first and asks whether it can be carried and reused. A target grammar may enlarge and thereby expose a local decision-sufficiency failure, but we do not claim the independently optimized closed-versus-open minimum-interface separation as a result of the present transport theory.

Across these literatures, the boundary is intentionally narrow. Exact aggregation, constrained coarsest refinement, user-specified initial partitions, causal transport, regime classification, adaptive management, and generic path dependence are established subjects. The distinctive source-relative sequence here is
\[
\text{carriage consistency}
\longrightarrow
\text{composition along a fixed route}
\longrightarrow
\text{coherence across different routes}
\longrightarrow
\text{minimum historical completion}.
\]
Standard exact refinement enters only after that sequence has supplied one inherited target labeling.

The transport defect should be read accordingly. It is not another name for a novel target quotient and is not a standalone theorem. It is a diagnostic burden defined after established refinement machinery has found the smallest exact target distinction compatible with the inherited labeling. Its ecological use is to summarize how much of the old decision interface can be retained and to help compare candidate monitoring repairs.

\section{Operational framework}
A finite controlled ecological system is a tuple
\[
\mathcal{S}=(X,A,G,\delta,y),
\]
where $X$ is the state set, $A$ is the declared action set, $G$ is the legal finite action grammar, $\delta$ is the controlled transition rule, and $y$ is the observable output. A macro-law is a surjection $\pi:X\times G\to Q$.

\begin{definition}[Exact operational macro-law]
A macro-law is exact when states in the same macro-fiber have identical current outputs, identical legal-action rows, and successors carrying the same macro-label under every declared legal action.
\end{definition}

Structural change from a source system $\mathcal{S}_0$ to a target system $\mathcal{S}_1$ is represented by a declared relation
\[
R\subseteq (X_0\times G_0)\times (X_1\times G_1).
\]
Throughout the exact finite theory, a declared replacement relation covers every product state on both sides. It may be many-to-many and need not embed one state space into another.

\begin{definition}[Carried source semantics]
Let the source macro-law be $\pi_0$. The inherited label at a target product state $z_1$ is defined when all source states related to $z_1$ have the same accepted source label:
\[
\pi_R(z_1)=q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{\pi_0(z_0):(z_0,z_1)\in R\}=\{q\}.
\]
If this set contains two or more labels, the declared relation does not induce one carried source meaning at $z_1$.
\end{definition}

Thus source-label carriage has a local semantic obstruction before any target-dynamics refinement is attempted: one target configuration cannot inherit two different accepted source labels while preserving both meanings. When carriage is well defined, the target may still enlarge the legal action grammar, so newly legal actions can expose distinctions inside an inherited source fiber. That later failure concerns decision sufficiency of a well-defined carried label and is distinct from a carriage conflict.

\begin{proposition}[Compositional carried semantics]
Let $R$ carry a source system to an intermediate system and $S$ carry the intermediate system to a terminal system, with both declared relations covering every state on both sides. Direct carriage through the relational composite $R;S$ is well defined if and only if carriage through $R$ and then through $S$ is well defined. Whenever these conditions hold,
\[
\boxed{
(\pi_0)_{R;S}=(\pi_R)_S.
}
\]
\end{proposition}

The proof is elementary but important for interpretation. If a first-stage label conflict existed, coverage by $S$ would propagate the conflicting source labels to at least one terminal state and make direct carriage undefined. If a second-stage conflict existed after a valid first carry, the corresponding differently labelled source ancestors would likewise conflict in the composite. Conversely, sequentially unambiguous labels are necessarily unambiguous in the composite. By induction, parenthesization of any finite fixed route is irrelevant. Historical context is therefore not required because one replacement trajectory has been represented in several stages; it is required only when genuinely different declared routes induce different complete terminal inherited maps.

\section{Results}
We present the route-coherence conclusion first because it is the manuscript's principal formal result. Carriage composition establishes that each fixed route has one decomposition-independent inherited meaning whenever it is defined. We then compare those route-level maps at a common terminal system. Portability and standard refinement enter afterward as the target decision-sufficiency audit.

\subsection{Headline result: route coherence and minimum history completion}
Consider a rooted directed acyclic graph of declared replacement relations. Every root-to-terminal path composes its edge relations and, when label-consistent, carries the accepted source label map to the terminal system.

% Preserve established theorem numbering while presenting the contribution first.
\setcounter{theorem}{2}
\begin{theorem}[History coherence]
If every root-to-terminal path induces the same terminal carried label map, then a single route-independent carried label assignment exists. Because the terminal controlled system and inherited assignment are then the same for every route, any subsequent exact target audit, repaired partition, and transport defect are also route independent.
\end{theorem}

If terminal carried maps disagree, some terminal configuration receives incompatible inherited source labels under the declared histories. Hence no single route-free carried label map can preserve all of those path-specific inherited assignments. This semantic obstruction does not assert that the corresponding unlabeled final repaired partitions must necessarily differ after refinement.

\begin{theorem}[Minimum history completion]
When terminal carried maps disagree, one immutable history mode per distinct carried terminal map is necessary and sufficient to preserve all declared inherited meanings. Standard relative exact refinement on the resulting history slices then supplies the coarsest exact history-aware interface compatible with those meanings.
\end{theorem}

Thus raw path identity is not retained. Histories are remembered only to the extent that they assign different inherited operational meanings to the same terminal system. The theorem does not infer historical pathways from field data; it characterizes the minimum context required once those pathways, relations, and source labels are declared.

\subsection{Supporting audit: operational decision sufficiency}
\setcounter{theorem}{0}
\begin{theorem}[Operational portability]
For a finite exact source macro-law and a declared label-consistent structural-change relation, the carried target partition is exact if and only if current outputs, legal-action rows, and successor carried labels are constant inside every carried target fiber.
\end{theorem}

Ecologically, this is the decision-sufficiency test. The question is whether two configurations that inherit one management state remain interchangeable for the declared target observations and interventions. It does not search for an unconstrained target-only aggregation.

\subsection{Supporting infrastructure: local obstruction and established exact refinement}
\begin{proposition}[Local obstruction]
Failure of portability is witnessed by two target states in one inherited fiber that differ in current output, legal actions, or successor inherited labels under a declared action or finite legal future.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Unique coarsest source-relative repair]
Starting from the carried target partition and iteratively splitting fibers by output, legal-action row, and successor block yields a finite fixed point. The fixed point is exact and is the unique coarsest exact target partition refining the inherited labels.
\end{theorem}

This theorem is included to make the audit and executable workflow self-contained, not as a claim of a new coarsest-refinement result. Initial-partition coarsest refinement, fixed-point termination, and uniqueness are established in partition-refinement and abstract-interpretation theory \citep{PaigeTarjan1987,Fernandez1990,RanzatoTapparo2007}, and user-specified initial distinctions are already used in biological model reduction \citep{ArgyrisEtAl2023}. MLTR's ecological use of the fixed point is diagnostic: the new blocks identify exactly which distinctions were missing from the inherited state variable for the declared target decision.

\subsection{Supporting quantitative witness: source-relative repair burden}
\begin{definition}[Transport defect]
Let $Q_0$ be the source macrostate set and $Q_1^{\min}$ the repaired target macrostate set returned by the standard source-relative exact refinement. Define
\[
\Delta_{\#}=|Q_1^{\min}|-|Q_0|,
\qquad
\Delta_{K}=\log_2|Q_1^{\min}|-\log_2|Q_0|.
\]
\end{definition}

These quantities are diagnostic summaries, not standalone mathematical novelty claims. In management terms, a small defect means that the old monitoring system may remain useful after adding a limited diagnostic distinction, whereas a large defect indicates that structural change has destroyed much of the former decision compression. In the verified accumulating family with $m$ independently exposed binary distinctions,
\[
|Q_0|=2,\qquad |Q_1^{\min}|=2^m+1,
\]
so $\Delta_{\#}=2^m-1$. This is a finite witness family, not a universal ecological scaling law or a claim that block-count increase is a new fundamental information quantity.

\section{Worked ecological example: restoration priority after pollinator turnover}
Consider two degraded sites, A and B, containing the same focal plant and currently showing the same coarse pollination output. Under the source monitoring and management repertoire, the only relevant distinction is whether adequate pollination is maintained without intervention. Sites A and B therefore occupy the same functional macrostate: both are classified as ``pollination maintained,'' and their future labels coincide under the source actions. A third state represents failure of pollination service. In the verified finite witness, the inherited target labels are $(0,0,1)$.

A restoration program must choose which site to treat first. Under the inherited macro-law, A and B are predicted to respond identically, so the decision is made using treatment cost alone. Suppose competitor removal is cheaper at A. The inherited model therefore recommends A as the first restoration target. This is a genuine management conclusion produced by the coarse variable, not merely a descriptive grouping.

Structural change then alters the pollinator community. The original dominant pollinator is lost, but one target configuration retains access to a substitute guild whereas the other does not. Competitor removal is also introduced as a new legal action because managers can now open floral access around the focal plant. Under this target-only intervention, the two inherited states have different successors: at A, removal does not recover pollination because no substitute response channel is present; at B, removal releases access to the substitute guild and pollination is maintained. The inherited equality of A and B is therefore false for the decision now being made.

The portability criterion detects the failure without requiring the entire target model to be discarded. The local witness is the pair of target states carrying the same inherited label but producing different successor labels under competitor removal. Standard exact refinement splits only that inherited fiber, changing $(0,0,1)$ to $(0,1,2)$ while preserving all unaffected inherited distinctions. The transport defect is one additional macrostate. Ecologically, the split identifies one field-relevant variable to add to monitoring: whether a site possesses a substitute-pollinator response channel that can be activated by the new intervention. The recommendation is therefore not to measure every interaction in the target community, but to measure the distinction that reverses the management response.

The management recommendation now reverses. Under the inherited law, A was preferred because treatment was cheaper and predicted outcomes were tied. Under the repaired law, B is preferred because only B retains the response channel needed for intervention success. The audit therefore changes the decision from ``treat the cheaper apparently equivalent site'' to ``treat the site whose hidden interaction structure supports recovery.'' Figure~\ref{fig:local-split} summarizes the workflow from inherited aggregation to diagnostic failure, exact refinement, monitoring redesign, and revised restoration priority.

The same example also clarifies the historical result. Suppose the terminal community can be reached either by pollinator loss followed by substitute colonization or by habitat modification followed by loss of the original guild. Compositional carriage guarantees that each fixed route has the same inherited meaning whether it is represented as one composite replacement or as its successive stages. The remaining question is between routes. If both routes carry the same complete terminal labels, the management variable is route independent. If the routes assign incompatible inherited meanings to the same terminal configurations, no route-free label assignment exists. The minimum exact completion then retains one immutable mode for each distinct carried terminal map. Historical context is therefore added only when it changes the operational meaning of the terminal state.

\section{Discussion}
The central ecological result is that validity of a state classification depends on the decision interface in which it is used. A state that was sufficient before structural change can become insufficient after species turnover, interaction rewiring, or expansion of the management repertoire, even if its descriptive label still appears sensible. The relevant audit is therefore not only whether the target process can be reduced somehow, but whether the state variable that managers already use still separates configurations that imply different feasible actions or recovery responses.

The formal route result sharpens when historical context is actually required. Carried semantics through one fixed chain is compositional: direct and sequential transport are simultaneously defined and yield the same inherited terminal map. Stepwise representation of one route is therefore not path dependence. Across genuinely different declared histories, the complete carried terminal maps---not raw path identities---are the exact units of historical context. Equal maps share one route-independent inherited law; unequal maps cannot be preserved by one mode, and exactly one immutable mode per equality class is sufficient.

The plant--pollinator example shows why this boundary changes an ecological decision. Before turnover, the inherited functional state supports a defensible rule: if two sites are equivalent in expected response, prioritize the cheaper intervention. After turnover and expansion of the management repertoire, that same rule directs restoration toward the wrong site because the coarse state suppresses access to a substitute pollinator guild. The local obstruction does more than say that the old partition is formally unstable. It names the contrast responsible for the decision reversal, making substitute-response capacity a concrete target for monitoring redesign.

The supporting refinement result should be interpreted in that limited role. Computing the unique coarsest stable refinement from an initial partition is established machinery, as are finite split witnesses and fixed-point termination. MLTR adds neither a new generic refinement algorithm nor a new general existence theorem. What the ecological audit contributes is the inherited target labeling to which that machinery is applied and the interpretation of the returned split: a distinction is retained only when the declared target outputs or interventions make it operationally consequential.

Transport defect has the same supporting status. It measures how much additional state information must be retained before the old decision interface becomes exact again. A defect of one, as in the worked example, suggests that an existing monitoring program may be salvaged by adding one targeted variable. A large defect signals that the old aggregation has lost much of its decision value and that incremental patching may no longer be efficient. The defect is not itself a monetary cost, empirical effect size, or new universal information invariant; its use is comparative and diagnostic within a declared source-relative audit.

The framework also offers a conservative principle for ecological history. Legacy effects and historical contingency can matter, but retaining every past event causes the state representation to expand unnecessarily. Here history is discarded whenever it leaves the complete carried operational meaning unchanged and is retained only when alternative declared routes assign different meanings to the present terminal configurations. This places the theory between two extremes: assuming that present configuration always erases history and treating every distinct historical path as a distinct ecological state.

\subsection{Scope and when not to use the framework}
The framework is appropriate when investigators can declare a finite or finitely represented source system, a target system, the outputs and interventions that matter, and a defensible relation carrying source labels to target configurations. Concrete uses include auditing restoration-priority classes after interaction turnover, redesigning adaptive-monitoring indicators when new interventions become available, testing whether habitat or resilience categories remain decision-sufficient after disturbance, and checking whether a reduced occupancy or community-state interface can be transferred to a structurally changed site before its parameters are re-estimated.

It should not be used as a substitute for estimating transition parameters, detecting species, inferring causal effects, identifying the source--target relation, or quantifying observation and model uncertainty. It is also not sufficient when approximate predictive error is acceptable but exact closure fails, because the present results return a structural obstruction rather than an error tolerance. When the declared state space, replacement relation, or action grammar is scientifically disputed, that uncertainty must be resolved or modeled separately before the audit can support a management decision.

The plant--pollinator case is a finite decision example, not evidence that a particular restoration program or species interaction will exhibit the stated reversal. Its purpose is to show how a management recommendation can change when a target intervention exposes a distinction hidden by an inherited functional class and how the separating witness can be translated into one additional monitoring target.

Several extensions follow naturally, but they should be evaluated by ecological value rather than theorem count. Approximate portability would allow bounded predictive error; stochastic versions would connect repair to probabilistic transitions and imperfect observations; and data-estimated structural relations would permit empirical uncertainty to enter the audit. A particularly important applied extension is to test whether candidate field variables actually recover the formal obstruction with lower measurement burden than full target-state reconstruction.

The main ecological implication is therefore not another aggregation algorithm. Ecological states are reusable decision interfaces only while they preserve the distinctions exposed by the current structure and intervention repertoire. MLTR first tests whether inherited source meaning can be carried consistently, then whether it remains decision-sufficient, and finally whether genuinely different replacement routes force historical context. Standard refinement is used only to identify the additional terminal distinctions required once those source-relative questions have been settled.

\section{Methods and proof organization}
The proof architecture follows the conceptual order of the audit. Carried-label consistency is a finite fiber condition on the declared source--target relation. Compositionality follows by showing that a label conflict on either stage is equivalent to a conflict in the composite relation, while sequentially unambiguous labels give the same pointwise terminal map as direct carriage. Route coherence then compares the complete maps induced by genuinely different root-to-terminal composites, and minimum history completion quotients those histories by equality of their maps. The operational portability test factors target outputs, legal-action rows, and successor inherited labels through the carried partition. Finally, established finite partition-refinement machinery computes the coarsest exact refinement of that partition. Detailed proofs, algorithm specifications, executable certificates, and reproducibility commands are provided in the supplementary manuscript.
