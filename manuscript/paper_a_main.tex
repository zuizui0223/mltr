\begin{abstract}
An ecological macro-law accepted at one stage need not retain one route-independent meaning after structural replacement. We formulate finite operational models in which declared replacement histories carry a source law to a terminal system. A single carried terminal law exists exactly when the complete carried terminal label maps agree across histories. When they disagree, histories can share an immutable mode exactly when their complete carried maps are equal; consequently, one mode per distinct map is necessary and sufficient to preserve all declared inherited meanings. Relative exact refinement on these history slices then yields the unique coarsest exact history-aware interface. Exact portability, local failure witnesses, and source-relative fixed-point repair supply the supporting audit for each route: they determine whether inherited labels remain exact and which target distinctions must be restored. Standard exactness and partition refinement are therefore infrastructure, while the central result locates the precise boundary between route-free reuse and mandatory historical context. A finite plant--pollinator example shows how structural replacement can reverse a restoration priority and when different turnover routes can or cannot share one management law.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} ecological model transfer; structural replacement; route coherence; historical context; coarse graining; adaptive management

\section{Introduction}
Ecological models rarely retain every species identity, interaction, demographic state, and management history. Instead, they work with compressed variables: occupancy classes, functional guilds, successional stages, resilience categories, habitat states, or other macrostates chosen because they preserve the distinctions needed for a particular prediction. This compression is not merely convenient. It is what makes ecological forecasting, monitoring, and intervention design possible when the underlying system is too large, too heterogeneous, or too poorly observed to model in full detail.

The difficulty is that ecological systems do not remain structurally fixed. Species turn over, interaction networks rewire, habitats are replaced, and management itself changes the set of actions that can be applied. Under such change, a macrostate that was once sufficient can silently become insufficient. Two configurations previously treated as equivalent may respond differently to a newly available intervention, may permit different future actions, or may lead to different successor states. A model can therefore remain internally consistent and empirically familiar while no longer supporting the management conclusion for which it is being reused.

This creates a practical ecological question that is distinct from ordinary model fitting:
\begin{quote}
Given a macro-law already accepted for a source system, when do all declared replacement histories preserve one terminal meaning, and what exact state or historical context is forced when they do not?
\end{quote}
The question arises whenever a reduced model is transferred across sites, time periods, restoration scenarios, disturbance histories, or altered interaction networks. It is especially acute in adaptive management \citep{Holling1978,Walters1986}, because a change in the intervention repertoire can expose distinctions that were irrelevant under the original management regime. The relevant failure is structural rather than statistical: even with perfect knowledge of the declared systems, the inherited variable may no longer factor the target dynamics.

Several mature literatures address neighboring problems, but not this constrained ecological transfer problem. Coarse graining and lumpability ask whether a partition is exact within a specified dynamical or stochastic system \citep{KemenySnell1960,FeretEtAl2012}. Bisimulation and partition-refinement methods identify behaviorally equivalent states and construct coarsest valid quotients \citep{Milner1989,LarsenSkou1991,PaigeTarjan1987}. Transportability asks whether causal conclusions transfer across environments \citep{PearlBareinboim2014}. Resilience and regime-shift theory characterize persistence, thresholds, and qualitative change \citep{Holling1973,SchefferEtAl2001}, while adaptive-management frameworks update decisions as evidence and system states change \citep{Holling1978,Walters1986}. These approaches are indispensable, but they do not by themselves answer whether an \emph{inherited} ecological macro-law remains exact after a declared structural replacement, nor do they identify the unique least repair constrained to preserve every inherited merge that remains valid.

We formulate that problem in a finite operational setting. A source macro-law is carried to a target system through a declared structural-change relation, which may connect non-nested state spaces. Exact portability is then tested against three ecological requirements: states sharing an inherited label must have the same current output, admit the same legal management actions, and lead under those actions to successors with the same inherited labels. This formulation makes the management repertoire part of the ecological model rather than an external afterthought. A classification can be adequate for observation alone yet fail once a new intervention is considered.

The headline result concerns replacement histories. All declared paths support one route-independent inherited terminal law exactly when their complete carried terminal label maps agree. If they disagree, the equality classes of those maps are the exact units of historical context: paths can share a mode if and only if their complete carried maps coincide, so one immutable mode per distinct map is necessary and sufficient. Three supporting results make this conclusion operational. A necessary-and-sufficient portability criterion tests inherited labels on each route; a finite local witness identifies failure; and standard fixed-point refinement, initialized by the carried labels, returns the unique coarsest exact source-relative repair and its transport defect. These supporting results are infrastructure for deciding whether route disagreement is semantic, terminal-dynamical, or absent.

The ecological value of these results is diagnostic rather than merely classificatory. The framework separates three cases that are often conflated in practice: an inherited variable remains valid; it fails but can be repaired by retaining one additional ecological distinction; or it becomes strongly path dependent and requires explicit historical context. In each case the output is a decision about model reuse, not simply a new partition. The plant--pollinator example developed below illustrates this distinction: a functional classification adequate under the original management regime recommends one restoration priority, whereas a target-only intervention exposes a hidden response channel and changes which site should be prioritized.

Our claims are deliberately limited. We do not propose a new generic partition-refinement algorithm, claim literature-firstness for path dependence, or infer structural-change relations and histories from data. Exactness is used as a benchmark for isolating structural failure before sampling error, parameter uncertainty, or approximate prediction are introduced. The contribution is a source-relative theory of ecological model reuse whose distinctive conclusion is exact: accepted macro-laws need not survive structural replacement as route-free laws, and route disagreement requires distinct immutable history modes precisely by equality classes of complete carried terminal maps.

\subsection{Coarse graining and lumpability}
Coarse graining is indispensable whenever ecological detail must be reduced to functional groups, occupancy states, trophic compartments, or other aggregate variables. Lumpability sharpens one part of this problem by asking when a proposed partition preserves a process exactly within a fixed system \citep{KemenySnell1960,FeretEtAl2012}. Our portability criterion uses a related exactness standard, and our repair ultimately relies on refinement of state classes. The difference is the direction of the question. We do not begin with an unconstrained target system and search for any valid reduction. We begin with a source macro-law whose interpretation is already accepted, carry that interpretation to a structurally changed target, and require every repaired target class to remain inside an inherited class. A target-only optimum may therefore be mathematically smaller while being inadmissible because it abandons the source meaning that managers intended to reuse.

\subsection{Bisimulation, abstraction, and partition refinement}
Bisimulation and related abstraction theories characterize states that agree in observations and future behavior \citep{Milner1989,LarsenSkou1991}. Coarsest-quotient and partition-refinement algorithms are established tools for constructing such equivalences \citep{PaigeTarjan1987}. We therefore do not claim novelty for quotient construction or iterative splitting. Their role here is machinery for a different constrained object: the unique coarsest exact partition among those that refine the labels carried from the source. Structural change may also be non-nested, so the problem is not limited to adding a component or action to one fixed state space. The substantive result is that failure of inherited semantics has a finite operational witness and that standard refinement, when initialized by the carried labels, yields a canonical least repair rather than an arbitrary new abstraction.

\subsection{Transportability and model transfer}
Transportability research asks whether conclusions learned in one environment remain valid in another, with causal identifiability and external validity as central concerns \citep{PearlBareinboim2014}. Our framework addresses a prior structural question. It assumes that the source and target systems and their relation have been declared and asks whether the inherited macrostate interface can, even in principle, support target outputs and interventions. It neither estimates the relation nor guarantees empirical transfer. A model may pass the structural audit and still transfer poorly because parameters or observations are uncertain; conversely, no amount of parameter refitting can make an inherited aggregation exact when a target action sends two states in one inherited class to different successor classes. Thus structural portability is complementary to, rather than a substitute for, statistical or causal transport analysis.

\subsection{Resilience, regime shifts, and ecological state classifications}
Resilience and regime-shift theory focus on persistence, thresholds, alternative stable states, and changes in qualitative system behavior \citep{Holling1973,SchefferEtAl2001}. These literatures often motivate the ecological macrostates that managers actually monitor, such as desirable and degraded regimes or classes of recovery potential. Our question begins one level earlier: after species turnover, rewiring, or intervention change, do those regime labels still contain enough information to predict the target actions under consideration? We do not introduce a new definition of resilience or a new threshold criterion. Instead, the framework can audit whether a resilience class or regime label remains operationally meaningful and, when it does not, identify the least distinction needed within that class. In the plant--pollinator example, the repaired distinction is not a new regime name imposed from scratch; it is substitute-response capacity revealed inside a formerly homogeneous functional state.

\subsection{Adaptive management}
Adaptive management treats intervention as an iterative decision problem in which policies are revised as evidence accumulates \citep{Holling1978,Walters1986}. Our framework is compatible with that goal but solves a different layer of the problem. Adaptive decision procedures generally require a state representation on which action consequences can be evaluated. We ask whether an inherited representation remains sufficient after the action repertoire or ecological structure changes. A local obstruction identifies which apparently equivalent states respond differently to a newly legal action; the unique coarsest exact repair identifies the smallest additional variable that must enter monitoring before the decision policy can be trusted again. The framework therefore audits and repairs the information interface supplied to adaptive management, rather than replacing policy optimization, learning, or uncertainty analysis.

\subsection{Relation to open-future compression bounds}
A neighboring but distinct problem asks whether each fixed closed future grammar may admit its own small optimal exact interface while the minimum exact interface for a jointly open future grammar is necessarily much larger. That question optimizes the closed interfaces independently and therefore does not fix one inherited source partition. The present paper has the opposite constraint: one accepted source macro-law is fixed first, carried to the target, and every admissible repair must refine its inherited labels. A target grammar may enlarge and thereby expose a local failure witness, but we do not claim the independent closed-versus-open minimum-interface separation as a result of the present transport theory.

Across these literatures, the boundary is intentionally narrow. The paper does not claim that exact aggregation, behavioral equivalence, model transfer, resilience analysis, adaptive decision-making, or open-future interface lower bounds are new here. Its contribution is to connect the first five through one source-relative ecological question: whether a previously accepted macro-law survives declared structural change and, if not, how to preserve as much inherited meaning as exact target prediction permits.

The novelty is therefore not the refinement routine itself. If any exact target quotient were admissible, standard refinement could return a target abstraction without preserving the meaning of the source variable, and there would be no canonical sense in which that abstraction was a repair of the inherited law. The carried source partition supplies both the admissible set and the baseline against which information loss is measured. Consequently, the transport defect is not another name for target quotient size: it is defined only after the unique coarsest exact refinement constrained by inherited semantics has been identified. Removing that source-relative constraint removes the canonical repair, the defect, and the associated decision about whether the old monitoring interface can be retained.

\section{Operational framework}
A finite controlled ecological system is a tuple
\[
\mathcal{S}=(X,A,G,\delta,y),
\]
where $X$ is the state set, $A$ is the declared action set, $G$ is the legal finite action grammar, $\delta$ is the controlled transition rule, and $y$ is the observable output. A macro-law is a surjection $\pi:X\times G\to Q$.

\begin{definition}[Exact operational macro-law]
A macro-law is exact when states in the same macro-fiber have identical current outputs, identical legal-action rows, and successors carrying the same macro-label under every declared legal action.
\end{definition}

Structural change from a source system $\mathcal{S}_0$ to a target system $\mathcal{S}_1$ is represented by a declared total relation
\[
R\subseteq (X_0\times G_0)\times (X_1\times G_1).
\]
The source law induces a carried target partition $\pi_R$. The target may enlarge the legal action grammar, so newly legal actions can expose distinctions inside an inherited source fiber. Here such grammar enlargement is treated only as a source-relative transport instance: the inherited source law remains fixed and constrains admissible target repair. We do not address the different cross-grammar problem in which closed interfaces are independently optimized before comparison with a jointly open minimum.

\section{Results}
We present the route-coherence conclusion first because it is the manuscript's principal result. The portability and refinement statements that support it follow in proof-dependency order. The established theorem numbers are retained so that the main text remains synchronized with the proof package and replay contracts.

\subsection{Headline result: route coherence and minimum history completion}
Consider a rooted directed acyclic graph of declared replacement relations. Every root-to-terminal path carries a source label map to the terminal system.

% Preserve established theorem numbering while presenting the contribution first.
\setcounter{theorem}{2}
\begin{theorem}[History coherence]
If every root-to-terminal path induces the same terminal carried label map, then a single route-independent carried label assignment, relative repaired partition, and transport defect exist.
\end{theorem}

If terminal carried maps disagree, some terminal configuration receives incompatible inherited source labels under the declared histories. Hence no single route-free carried label map can preserve all of those path-specific inherited assignments. This semantic obstruction does not assert that the corresponding unlabeled final repaired partitions must necessarily differ after refinement.

\begin{theorem}[Minimum history completion]
When terminal carried maps disagree, one immutable history mode per distinct carried terminal map is necessary and sufficient to preserve all declared histories. Relative exact refinement on the augmented system yields the unique coarsest exact history-aware interface.
\end{theorem}

Thus path identity is retained exactly to the extent required to preserve incompatible inherited terminal meanings. The theorem does not infer historical pathways from field data; it characterizes the minimum context required once those pathways, relations, and inherited label semantics are declared.

\subsection{Supporting infrastructure: operational portability criterion}
\setcounter{theorem}{0}
\begin{theorem}[Operational portability]
For a finite exact source macro-law and a declared label-consistent structural-change relation, the carried target partition is exact if and only if current outputs, legal-action rows, and successor carried labels are constant inside every carried target fiber.
\end{theorem}

This theorem provides a direct diagnostic for whether a variable learned before structural change still supports every declared target prediction and intervention. It is source-relative: the target partition is tested as inherited, rather than replaced by an unrelated abstraction optimized only for the target.

\subsection{Supporting infrastructure: local obstruction and unique coarsest repair}
\begin{proposition}[Local obstruction]
Failure of portability is witnessed by two target states in one inherited fiber that differ in current output, legal actions, or successor inherited labels under a declared action or finite legal future.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Unique coarsest source-relative repair]
Starting from the carried target partition and iteratively splitting fibers by output, legal-action row, and successor block yields a finite fixed point. The fixed point is exact and is the unique coarsest exact target partition refining the inherited labels.
\end{theorem}

The result does more than reject transfer. It identifies the minimum additional ecological distinction required to recover exact prediction while retaining every inherited merge that remains operationally valid. Standard partition-refinement machinery supplies the construction; the substantive claim is its source-relative minimality and uniqueness under structural transport.

\subsection{Supporting quantitative witness: source-relative repair burden}
\begin{definition}[Transport defect]
Let $Q_0$ be the source macrostate set and $Q_1^{\min}$ the unique repaired target macrostate set. Define
\[
\Delta_{\#}=|Q_1^{\min}|-|Q_0|,
\qquad
\Delta_{K}=\log_2|Q_1^{\min}|-\log_2|Q_0|.
\]
\end{definition}

These quantities measure the minimum burden of preserving inherited ecological meaning. In management terms, a small defect means that the old monitoring system can remain useful after adding a limited diagnostic distinction, whereas a large defect means that structural change has invalidated much of the former compression and therefore much of the old decision interface. In the verified accumulating family with $m$ independently exposed binary distinctions,
\[
|Q_0|=2,\qquad |Q_1^{\min}|=2^m+1,
\]
so $\Delta_{\#}=2^m-1$. This is a sharp finite witness, not a universal ecological scaling law. Its role is to show that apparently modest structural change can destroy most of the original compression when target operations independently expose previously merged coordinates.

\section{Worked ecological example: restoration priority after pollinator turnover}
Consider two degraded sites, A and B, containing the same focal plant and currently showing the same coarse pollination output. Under the source monitoring and management repertoire, the only relevant distinction is whether adequate pollination is maintained without intervention. Sites A and B therefore occupy the same functional macrostate: both are classified as ``pollination maintained,'' and their future labels coincide under the source actions. A third state represents failure of pollination service. In the verified finite witness, the inherited target labels are $(0,0,1)$.

A restoration program must choose which site to treat first. Under the inherited macro-law, A and B are predicted to respond identically, so the decision is made using treatment cost alone. Suppose competitor removal is cheaper at A. The inherited model therefore recommends A as the first restoration target. This is a genuine management conclusion produced by the coarse variable, not merely a descriptive grouping.

Structural change then alters the pollinator community. The original dominant pollinator is lost, but one target configuration retains access to a substitute guild whereas the other does not. Competitor removal is also introduced as a new legal action because managers can now open floral access around the focal plant. Under this target-only intervention, the two inherited states have different successors: at A, removal does not recover pollination because no substitute response channel is present; at B, removal releases access to the substitute guild and pollination is maintained. The inherited equality of A and B is therefore false for the decision now being made.

The portability criterion detects the failure without requiring the entire target model to be discarded. The local witness is the pair of target states carrying the same inherited label but producing different successor labels under competitor removal. Source-relative refinement splits only that inherited fiber, changing $(0,0,1)$ to $(0,1,2)$ while preserving all unaffected inherited merges. The transport defect is one additional macrostate. Ecologically, the repaired state variable adds exactly one field-relevant distinction: whether a site possesses a substitute-pollinator response channel that can be activated by the new intervention.

The management recommendation now reverses. Under the inherited law, A was preferred because treatment was cheaper and predicted outcomes were tied. Under the repaired law, B is preferred because only B retains the response channel needed for intervention success. The theorem therefore changes the decision from ``treat the cheaper apparently equivalent site'' to ``treat the site whose hidden interaction structure supports recovery.'' Figure~\ref{fig:local-split} summarizes the complete workflow from inherited aggregation to diagnostic failure, unique coarsest exact repair, and revised restoration priority.

The same example also clarifies the historical result. Suppose the terminal community can be reached either by pollinator loss followed by substitute colonization or by habitat modification followed by loss of the original guild. If both routes carry the same terminal labels, the repaired management variable is route independent. If the routes assign incompatible inherited meanings to the same terminal configurations, no route-free label assignment exists. The minimum exact completion then retains one immutable mode for each distinct carried terminal map. Historical context is therefore added only when it changes the operational meaning of the terminal state.

\section{Discussion}
The central result is that acceptance of a macro-law at the source does not guarantee one route-free meaning after structural replacement. Across declared histories, the complete carried terminal maps---not raw path identities---are the exact units of historical context. Equal maps share one route-independent law; unequal maps cannot be preserved by one mode, and exactly one immutable mode per equality class is sufficient. The theorem therefore distinguishes necessary historical context from indiscriminate retention of full histories.

The plant--pollinator example shows why this boundary is an ecological decision problem rather than a purely formal property. Before turnover, the inherited functional state supports a defensible rule: if two sites are equivalent in expected response, prioritize the cheaper intervention. After turnover and expansion of the management repertoire, that same rule directs restoration toward the wrong site because the coarse state suppresses access to a substitute pollinator guild. The practical failure occurs even though the old labels remain interpretable and both sites may still look similar under routine monitoring. If alternative turnover routes also carry different inherited meanings to the same terminal configurations, the repaired state must additionally retain the corresponding map class; if their maps agree, no historical variable is required by this contract.

The supporting portability and repair results offer a staged audit. First, they ask whether the inherited monitoring variable remains sufficient for the target actions. Second, when it does not, they return a finite witness that can be translated into an ecological contrast to measure or record. Third, they identify the unique coarsest exact refinement of the carried labels. In the example, managers need not rebuild the model around every species and interaction; they need only distinguish sites by substitute-response capacity. Fixed-system exactness and partition-refinement machinery make this audit executable, but they are not the paper's headline contribution.

Transport defect then has a direct management interpretation. It measures how much additional state information must be retained before the old decision interface becomes valid again. A defect of one, as in the worked example, suggests that an existing monitoring program may be salvaged by adding one targeted variable. A large defect signals that the old aggregation has lost most of its decision value and that incremental patching may no longer be efficient. The defect is not itself a monetary cost or an empirical effect size, but it can organize comparisons among candidate monitoring schemes by quantifying the minimum structural information each must recover.

Route coherence is the organizing ecological conclusion, not an ancillary observation. Restoration outcomes can depend on whether a community reached its present configuration through disturbance, colonization, management, or gradual turnover, but the theory does not assume that history always matters. It identifies when path-specific inherited semantics cannot be represented by one route-free label map and proves the necessary-and-sufficient completion. This prevents both extremes---discarding path information automatically and retaining full histories when they do not alter the inherited semantic assignment.

\subsection{Scope and when not to use the framework}
The framework is appropriate when investigators can declare a finite or finitely represented source system, a target system, the outputs and interventions that matter, and a defensible relation carrying source labels to target configurations. Concrete uses include auditing restoration-priority classes after interaction turnover, redesigning adaptive-monitoring indicators when new interventions become available, testing whether habitat or resilience categories remain decision-sufficient after disturbance, and checking whether a reduced occupancy or community-state interface can be transferred to a structurally changed site before its parameters are re-estimated.

It should not be used as a substitute for estimating transition parameters, detecting species, inferring causal effects, identifying the source--target relation, or quantifying observation and model uncertainty. It is also not sufficient when approximate predictive error is acceptable but exact closure fails, because the present results return a structural obstruction rather than an error tolerance. When the declared state space or action grammar is itself scientifically disputed, that uncertainty must be resolved or modeled separately before the portability result can support a management decision.

The plant--pollinator case is a finite decision example, not evidence that a particular restoration program or species interaction will exhibit the stated reversal. Its purpose is to show how a management recommendation can change when a target intervention exposes a distinction hidden by an inherited functional class.

Several extensions follow naturally, but they should be evaluated by ecological value rather than theorem count. Approximate portability would allow bounded predictive error; stochastic versions would connect repair to probabilistic transitions and imperfect observations; and data-estimated structural relations would permit empirical uncertainty to enter the audit. The worked example also suggests an applied workflow in which field measurements are chosen from the local obstruction: once competitor removal reveals substitute-response capacity as the missing distinction, monitoring can be redesigned specifically to estimate that trait or interaction channel.

The main ecological implication is therefore not another aggregation algorithm. An accepted macro-law can fail to survive structural replacement as a route-free law. When it does, complete carried terminal maps determine exactly which histories may share a state and how many immutable modes are required; the unique coarsest exact repair then adds only the terminal distinctions forced by the declared operational model.

\section{Methods and proof organization}
Proofs proceed by operational factorization and finite monotone partition refinement. The portability theorem follows by necessity and sufficiency of output, legal-row, and successor uniformity. Local obstruction is the contrapositive witness. Finite refinement terminates because every nontrivial update strictly refines a partition of a finite set; exactness holds at the fixed point, and any exact refinement of the carried labels must refine every intermediate partition, proving coarseness and uniqueness. History minimality follows because two paths can share one immutable mode exactly when their complete carried terminal maps agree. Detailed proof dependencies, algorithm specifications, and reproducibility commands are provided in the supplementary manuscript.
