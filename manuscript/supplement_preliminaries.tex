\section{Mathematical preliminaries}

\subsection{Finite operational systems}
A finite controlled ecological system is a tuple
\[
\mathcal{S}=(X,A,G,\delta,y),
\]
where $X$ is a finite state set, $A$ is a declared action alphabet, $G$ is the legal finite action grammar, $\delta$ is the controlled successor rule, and $y$ is the declared output. The grammar is part of the model: two states may be observationally identical yet operationally distinct if they admit different actions or different legal futures.

A macro-law is represented by a surjection $\pi:X\times G\to Q$. Its fibers are ecological macrostates used for prediction and intervention. Exactness requires equality of current output, legal-action rows, and successor macro-labels within every fiber.

\subsection{Structural change and carried source semantics}
Let $\mathcal{S}_0$ and $\mathcal{S}_1$ be source and target systems. A declared replacement relation
\[
R\subseteq (X_0\times G_0)\times(X_1\times G_1)
\]
is assumed in the exact finite theory to cover every product state on both sides. The relation may be many-to-many and non-nested: turnover, replacement, or rewiring need not embed the source state space into the target.

Let $\pi_0$ be the accepted source label map. A carried target label is defined at target product state $z_1$ only when all source states related to $z_1$ have one source-label value:
\[
\pi_R(z_1)=q
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{\pi_0(z_0):(z_0,z_1)\in R\}=\{q\}.
\]
Coverage makes this set nonempty. If it contains multiple labels, carriage is undefined at $z_1$ and the differently labelled source ancestors form a finite source-semantic conflict witness.

\subsection{Composition of replacement relations}
For consecutive relations
\[
R\subseteq Z_0\times Z_1,
\qquad
S\subseteq Z_1\times Z_2,
\]
write
\[
R;S=\{(z_0,z_2):\exists z_1,\ z_0Rz_1\land z_1Sz_2\}.
\]
Under the total-coverage contract used here, direct carriage through $R;S$ is defined if and only if sequential carriage through $R$ and then $S$ is defined. When defined,
\[
(\pi_0)_{R;S}=(\pi_R)_S.
\]
Thus decomposition of one fixed replacement route cannot itself generate historical dependence.

\subsection{Target decision sufficiency and standard exact refinement}
Once one carried target labeling $\pi_R$ is defined, it is audited against the target system. States in one carried fiber must have identical current outputs, legal-action rows, and successor carried labels under every declared target action.

If the carried partition is not exact, the admissible repair class consists only of exact target partitions that refine $\pi_R$. Standard finite partition-refinement machinery returns the unique coarsest exact element of that class. This existence/coarseness/uniqueness pattern is established refinement theory; MLTR uses it after source-carried semantics has been fixed.

\subsection{Partition order}
For partitions $P$ and $P'$ of the same finite set, write $P'\preceq P$ when every block of $P'$ is contained in a block of $P$. Thus $P'$ is a refinement of $P$. The source-relative exact repair is the greatest element, under this order, among exact target partitions refining the carried partition.

\subsection{History-indexed transport}
For a rooted directed acyclic graph of declared structural changes, every label-consistent root-to-terminal path induces a complete carried terminal map. Fixed-route composition ensures that this map is independent of how the path is parenthesized or subdivided. Different declared paths are route coherent exactly when their complete terminal maps agree. When they disagree, an immutable history mode indexes the equality classes of distinct carried terminal maps before exact refinement is applied.
